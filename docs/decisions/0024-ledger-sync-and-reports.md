# 0024 Ledger sync and the balance sheet / profit and loss reports (Phase 4, task K2)

Status: accepted for the code, **provisional for every Exact name and meaning below: verify with credentials.** Nothing here has run against a real Exact division. Code: `packages/exact/src/client.ts` (`listGlAccountsChunk`, `listReportingBalancesChunk`, `listBudgetsChunk`), `apps/worker/src/exact/ledger-sync.ts`, `apps/worker/migrations/0010_ledger.sql`, `packages/core/src/reports/`, `apps/worker/src/read/ledger-routes.ts`. Supersedes the "per-period, caller sums" remark of ADR 0014 item 8 for the reports.

## What was built

- **Tables** (migration 0010): `gl_accounts` (the chart, `is_active`, never deleted), `gl_balances` (per account, year, period and `dimension`), `gl_budgets` (per account, year, period, `scenario`, `dimension`), `gl_sync_years` (last complete read per entity and year), and `sync_state.unavailable_until`. All money is integer minor units at the scale of the division currency (`currencyScale`; JPY 0, EUR 2, KWD 3), signed. The unique key of balances and budgets has a `dimension` (and `scenario`) on top of the requested `(division, account, year, period)`, because Exact may split a period over status or cost centre and keeping one row would silently under-count.
- **Sync** as three more entities after open items, relations and IBANs: `gl_accounts`, `gl_balances`, `gl_budgets`. FULL runs only; the hourly incremental run never reads ledger data (tested). A manual "Sync now" full covers them too (`readLedger: false` exists as a test/ops switch). They are part of the cycle: a cycle is complete, and `last_full_completed_at` is set, only when they are done.
- **Reports** as pure functions in `packages/core/src/reports/` and four read endpoints (below).

## Meaning of the numbers (assumptions)

1. **Sign.** `Amount` = debit - credit: assets and costs positive, liabilities, equity and revenue negative. Budgets use the same convention (`BUDGET_SIGN` in `reports/profit-loss.ts` flips it in one place if Exact stores natural amounts).
2. **Movement vs cumulative.** `LEDGER_BALANCE_SEMANTICS = "movement"` (`reports/ledger.ts`): a reporting balance row is the net movement of its period, and period 0 holds the brought-forward balance of balance sheet accounts. A balance sheet account at period P is the sum of periods 0..P; a profit and loss account year to date is the sum of periods 1..P. The alternative `"cumulative"` (the row already is the balance at that period; take the latest period at or before P) is implemented and tested, so a different finding is a one-line change. If period 0 does not exist the check line shows it (below).
3. **Reporting year** is the Amsterdam calendar year. A non-calendar financial year needs a setting (open question).
4. **Year end** for the comparison is all periods of the compare year (0..99, so extra closing periods 13+ are included). The compare year is the year before, unless `compareYear` is given.
5. **Currency.** Every ledger amount is in the division's base currency; the client reads it at `currencyScale(divisionCurrency)` through the raw-number JSON path, no float anywhere. Amounts beyond the safe integer range are refused (`BAD_SHAPE`), aggregates are summed as BigInt.
6. **Draft entries.** `Status` is kept in the row key but not filtered: whatever Exact includes in ReportingBalance is in the report (open question: only final entries?).
7. **Dimensions and split keys (review K2).** The row key is status, cost centre and cost unit (balances) or scenario, cost centre and cost unit (budgets). If Exact splits a figure over a field we do not read (item, project, ...), the same key arrives twice. Inside one chunk the parts are added. Across chunks the later part **overwrites** (the stored amount may then be too low), deliberately: we cannot tell a second part from the same page served twice by an offset `$skiptoken` after rows moved, and adding would then double-count a real figure. Either way the row gets `split_key = 1` (a re-read of the same chunk after a crash, same pass and chunk id, is not a split), the complete read records the count in `gl_sync_years.split_keys`, and the report responses carry `splitKeys` so a screen warns instead of showing a silently low figure. The fix is to add the field to the `$select` and the dimension.

## Account type to section mapping

Balance sheet (`BalanceType` B), `reports/classify.ts`:

| Source | Section |
|---|---|
| `Type` 10 (cash), 12 (bank), 20 (accounts receivable) | assets |
| `Type` 22 (accounts payable) | liabilities |
| else, **only when the account has a `BalanceSide`** (review K2), keywords in `TypeDescription` (English and Dutch), tested in this order: equity (equity, capital, reserve, retained, eigen vermogen, kapitaal, overgedragen), liabilities (payable, creditor, crediteur, loan, lening, liability, schuld, vat, btw, tax, belasting, accrual, provision, voorziening), assets (cash, bank, kas, receivable, debtor, debiteur, stock, inventory, voorraad, fixed assets, vaste activa, asset, activa, prepaid, vooruitbetaald, investment) | as listed |
| anything else | **unclassified** |
| side check: assets expect `BalanceSide` D, liabilities and equity C; a contradiction | **unclassified** |

`Type` 21 was mapped to liabilities by the builder; review K2 removed it because a second recollection has it as "prepayment accounts receivable", so it now falls through to the keyword rules. An unknown `Type` with no description, or a keyword without a side to confirm it, is always unclassified: a weaker signal never places an account in a section on its own. The type codes are a recollection and wrong ones are survivable: the side check and the "unclassified" line mean a bad guess shows up as its own line instead of moving money between sections. Profit and loss (`BalanceType` W): `BalanceSide` C = revenue, D = costs, none = unclassified. An account that is not in the chart (balances arrived before it, or it is gone) is kept as "Unknown account <id>", unclassified, with its balance type taken from its rows.

## Report builders

- `buildBalanceSheet(accounts, balances, {year, period, compareYear, compareToYearEnd})`: sections Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Unclassified; liabilities and equity are shown as positive balances (credit - debit), assets and unclassified as debit - credit; the year's result (credit - debit of the W accounts, a profit positive) is its own figure on the liabilities and equity side. **Check line** `difference = assets - (liabilities + equity + result) + unclassified`, which equals the plain sum of every account's signed balance: 0 for a complete ledger, and **surfaced, never hidden** otherwise (an opening that lacks last year's result, a period not yet synced, wrong semantics). It does **not** catch opening balances that are missing altogether (the year's movements still net to zero), so `hasOpeningBalances` says per column whether any period 0 row exists (review K2). Both columns (the year and the comparison) balance on their own because each year's own W accounts form that column's result.
- `buildProfitAndLoss(accounts, balances, budgets, {year, periodTo})`: year to date (periods 1..periodTo) actual, budget, and last year's same periods. **Sign:** every line is credit - debit, so revenue is positive, costs are negative and the result is the plain sum of the lines; a positive variance (`actual - comparison`) is always good for the result. Percentages are integer basis points of the absolute comparison figure, half away from zero, null when the comparison is 0. `budgets: null` (unavailable) makes every budget figure null, not 0; `compareYear: null` (that year never read completely) does the same for every last-year figure (review K2: before, an unread year showed 0 and a variance equal to the actual). A budget row for an account the chart does not know counts as profit and loss. Lines that are zero in every column are omitted; totals are unaffected. Tested on a realistic hand-built chart and two years of ledger (opening balances, a closing period 13, a contra account, a side contradicting its code), in both semantic modes (`reports-realistic.test.ts`): the balance sheet reconciles at every period of both years.
- Properties tested over 40 seeded random ledgers: section totals equal the sum of their lines, the result equals revenue + costs + unclassified, the check line equals the independent plain sum however accounts were classified, no account with a balance is dropped.

## Sync behaviour

- **Resumable like the open-item lists.** Each chunk (default 5 pages) writes its rows, the `$skiptoken` and the progress in one D1 batch, guarded by the sync lease; rows are upserted with the `run_seq` guard. A balances pass carries its year in the token (`<year>:<token>`); with one token per entity, a year whose pass can be resumed is read first so it is never overwritten by the other year's fresh pass.
- **Passes may outlive the cycle (review K2).** A full cycle is abandoned after 36 hours (`CYCLE_MAX_AGE_MS`): the scheduled budget (4 x 240 a night) then gives the ledger at most two nights, about 1,750 calls after the other entities. A ledger beyond that (2,000 accounts split over a few cost centres is enough) would restart every other night and never finish. So a saved ledger pass is resumed in a later cycle while it is younger than `LEDGER_PASS_MAX_AGE_MS` (5 days: nights 1 to 5, about 4,300 ledger calls; below the weekly refresh) and not restarted. `sync_state.pass_seq` (the run that started the pass) and `pass_started_at` are kept for it. Safety: "not seen" is judged against the pass's own start, not the cycle's (`last_seen_sync_id < pass_seq`), so a row the pass read days ago is never struck; a pass that ran over several runs needs two strikes; an older pass, or a restart, starts from page 1. The rest of the cycle (open items, relations, IBANs) still restarts every 36 hours as before, so those stay fresh while a large ledger finishes.
- **Cadence.** Chart: every cycle. Balances of the current year: every cycle. Balances of the previous year: when never read completely, when the last complete read was made while it was still the current year (so once more right after 1 January 00:00 Amsterdam, review K2: otherwise the last days of December waited up to a week), or the last complete read is 7 days old (we cannot see cheaply whether the year-end period changed, so "at most weekly"). Budgets of the current year: same weekly rule. A cycle that crosses the new year reads the new year's balances and budgets at once; on 1 January the new year usually has no figures yet: it is recorded as read with 0 rows and the API answers `no_data` for it, while the old year stays readable.
- **Not seen means not gone, until proven.** Only a COMPLETE read counts. Unseen rows get a strike (idempotent per pass through `strike_cycle` = `pass_seq`, so a crash between statements cannot double it); after two strikes (one when the pass ran in a single run, or with `allowLargeClose`) an account is set `is_active = 0` (never deleted) and a balance or budget row is deleted. An empty answer while we hold rows changes nothing and is **not** recorded as a complete read (review K2: before, it moved `completed_at`, so stale data looked fresh); the entity ends `ok` with `empty_answer_ignored` and the next cycle asks again. A genuinely empty ledger at the first sync (nothing held) is a complete read. Known cost of the two-strike rule: when a draft row (status 20) becomes processed (50) in a pass carried over several runs, the old row stays one more cycle and the figure is counted twice until then; single-run passes (the normal night) delete it at once. The close guard of the open items (more than `max(5, 20 %)` at once) applies; the entity then ends `error` / `close_guard` and an admin runs a full sync with `allowLargeClose`.
- **Budgets are optional.** If the first page of a fresh budget pass answers 400, 403 or 404 from Exact (not one of our gate's own 400 codes such as `division_mismatch` or a request-validation code, review K2), the entity ends `skipped` with `budgets_unavailable_<status>`, `sync_state.unavailable_until` is set a week ahead and nothing else is affected (the cycle completes, reports have no budget column). A manual full sync with `restart` tries again at once; a complete read clears the flag. A 400 on a later page (a stale token) or any 5xx is an ordinary failure.
- **Errors** follow the shared classification: budget exhausted, rate limit, time, subrequests and transient failures end `partial` and the next run resumes; auth failures stop with `needs_reauth`.

## Call arithmetic

Reference ledger: 300 accounts x 12 periods = 3,600 balance rows a year, 150 profit and loss accounts x 12 = 1,800 budget rows, 60 rows a page.

| Read | Calls | When |
|---|---|---|
| Chart | 300 / 60 = **5** | every night |
| Balances, current year | 3,600 / 60 = **60** | every night |
| Balances, previous year | **60** | once, then weekly |
| Budgets | 1,800 / 60 = **30** | once, then weekly |

A normal night adds 65 calls (70 if every account also has an opening row, 13 rows a year); the weekly night 155. With the reference division's open items (50 + 34 pages), IBANs (67), divisions and relations (2), a normal night is 218 calls, inside the 240-call budget of one run; the weekly night is 308, so it needs a second attempt, which the scheduler already gives (4 attempts a day, a partial full sync is retried after 5 minutes). **The scheduled worst case does not move: 4 x 240 + 25 x 40 = 1,960 of 5,000 per administration and day** (`test/ledger-budget.test.ts`; the job budget cap is unchanged). At pages of 1,000 the whole ledger is 1 + 4 + 4 + 2 = 11 calls. A very large ledger (2,000 accounts: 34 + 800 + 200 = 1,034 calls for the first read) spans two nights; with every period split over four cost centres it is 4,034 calls, which only finishes because the ledger passes may run for 5 days (above; `ledger-budget.test.ts` has the arithmetic). The second attempt of the weekly night resumes the previous year from its saved token: it reads neither the chart nor the current year nor the previous year's first pages again (`ledger-sync-review.test.ts`). Subrequests: every D1 batch of a chunk is metered (`runBatches`), as are the counts and the year lookups. The test `ledger-sync.test.ts` runs 300 accounts x 12 periods x 2 years plus budgets through the sync at both page sizes inside a 240-call run.

## API

All GET, any signed-in role, `?division=` validated against the readable divisions (404 for an unknown or disabled one), account ids resolved through their own division (404 otherwise), `no-store`. Types in `packages/core/src/api-types.ts`.

- `/api/reports/balance-sheet?year&period&compare=yearend|period|none[&compareYear]` (defaults: current Amsterdam year; the latest period with figures (`latestPeriodWithData`), capped at the current month, or at 12 for another year, and that calendar default when the year has no figure in periods 1+ or was never read completely (review K3b); `yearend`; year - 1). Periods 1..16. Both report responses carry `latestPeriodWithData` (one index lookup on `idx_gl_balances_year`).
- `/api/reports/profit-loss?year&periodTo[&scenario=<guid>|none]` (`periodTo` defaults like `period` above). The default scenario is the first (sorted) with rows for the year; `budgetScenarios` lists all. A compare year that was never read completely is excluded from the figures and flagged `compareMissing`.
- `/api/ledger/accounts` (chart with `isBlocked`, `isActive`, statement and section, `unclassifiedCount`) and `/api/ledger/accounts/:id/balances?year` (period rows, rows split by status or cost centre added; debit and credit are null when any row lacks them).
- **Empty state:** the balances of the requested year never read completely: 200 with `status: "not_synced"` and `report: null`; read completely but no row at all for the year: `status: "no_data"`, `report: null` (review K2: an empty sheet is never presented as a real one). **Freshness** per ledger entity (`lastCompletedAt`, `stale`, `staleAfterHours`): chart from `sync_state`, balances and budgets from `gl_sync_years` for the requested year, and `compareFreshness` for the compare year. Stale means never, or older than 36 hours for the current year's balances and the chart; **budgets and the balances of any other year use 8 days** because they are read weekly (a literal 36 hours would always flag them). `budgetsAvailable` is false while `unavailable_until` is in the future; budgets read completely but with no row for the year give no budget column (null figures), not zeros. `splitKeys` counts split keys in the data used (above). The chart answer holds at most 5,000 accounts (`truncated`); 2,000 accounts are about 700 kB. Queries per request: one D1 batch (state) plus one batch (chart and balances) plus one for budgets; the report queries use `idx_gl_balances_year` / the unique keys (EXPLAIN test in `ledger-api.test.ts`). Response shapes are pinned to the types like K1's (`ledger-api.test.ts`).
